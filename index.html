Throughput

A serving node is not a laptop.

Reviews benchmark these boxes one prompt at a time. A serving node runs hundreds of streams at once, and re-uses the same weights read for all of them. The difference is not marginal.

gpt-oss 120B · MXFP4 · DGX Spark
33 → 863 tok/s
Single stream versus aggregate throughput at 256 concurrent streams. A 26× gain from batching alone.
Dendro Logic concurrency benchmark, 2026
Nemotron Super 49B · NVFP4 · DGX Spark
5.8 → 695 tok/s
The same box, the same model, the same session — 120× more throughput once you feed it real concurrent demand.
Dendro Logic concurrency benchmark, 2026

We model 300 tok/s aggregate per unit — roughly a third of the benchmarked peak. Real fleets never sit at peak concurrency, and ROCm is less mature than CUDA. We would rather be wrong in the direction that costs us the deal than the one that costs you money.

Break-even fleet size

488 owned nodes —
or 1,937 sold ones.

Company overhead is the same whichever way the fleet is funded. Covering A$1.4M takes 488 company-owned nodes at A$2,604 each, or 1,937 customer-owned nodes at A$723. Capital buys a 4× shorter road to profitability — four times fewer households, installs and field visits to reach the same place.

Company operating cost of A$1.4M/yr assumes roughly 9 FTE — engineering, field operations, support, sales and admin — plus cloud infrastructure and G&A. The all-customer-owned column also earns ~A$0.70M of one-off hardware margin, not shown.

The moat

Free electricity isn't the moat.
The free building is.

Energy is only about a fifth of the cost of inference. If cheap power were the whole story, we would have a 20% edge and a hard fight. The real advantage is everything a data centre has to build that we simply don't.

What a data centre pays for
  • ×Land, and a planning fight to use it
  • ×The building, cooling plant and water
  • ×A grid connection — often a multi-year queue
  • ×Physical security, redundancy, network
  • ×Electricity at commercial rates, all day
  • ×Community consent it increasingly cannot get
What SunStack pays for
  • ✓A A$2,800 box and a A$400 install
  • ✓A revenue share to the homeowner
  • ✓Roughly A$30 a year of electricity
  • ✓Nothing else. The building, the cooling, the grid connection and the security already exist — and somebody else paid for all four.
The line that matters

Over 200× the value
from the same sunshine.

A unit consumes roughly 394 kWh a year. Exported to the grid at 4¢, those kilowatt-hours earn a household about A$16. Turned into tokens, the same energy produces A$4,044 of compute revenue. We are not competing for electrons — we are repricing them by two orders of magnitude.

Why these matter

Three of the four need little or no capital and reach the same households we are already visiting. They convert a hardware business into a services business, and they are what carries company overhead while the owned fleet is still being built.

The line we deliberately excluded

Grid and VPP services. The box absorbs surplus exactly where it is generated, and the market already licenses aggregated home batteries. It is real revenue — and it appears in none of the numbers in this deck. We will forecast it after a retailer trial, not before.

Validation, not threat

SCX.ai IPO'd at A$40M with ~A$5.4M contracted ARR and A$14.6M of unbilled contract value. Australian buyers are already paying a premium for onshore inference. That is our thesis, proven by someone else's order book.

Different cost floor

They compete on efficiency per token inside a building they must pay for. We have no building, no cooling plant and no grid connection to buy — and we add capacity in A$4,000 steps instead of A$40M ones.

Different failure mode

A data centre that overbuilds is stranded capital. A network that overbuilds simply stops signing homes that month. Our downside is slower growth, not a stranded asset.

Unproven — and exactly how we'll test it
  • →Paid utilisation. The biggest lever in the model. Test: a 90-day metered pilot with paying design partners — not letters of intent.
  • →The A$1.90/M blended price. Test: signed, invoiced usage across government, education and enterprise, showing what the sovereignty premium is actually worth.
  • →Sustained concurrency on our hardware. Benchmarks are for DGX Spark on CUDA. Test: reproduce them on Ryzen AI Max under ROCm before we buy at volume.
  • →Homeowner churn and hardware failure. Test: a 12-month cohort with real meter and support-hour data.
  • →Grid-service revenue. Test: a trial with a retailer or VPP aggregator. Excluded from every forecast until it clears.

Every number here is a hypothesis with a test attached. We already discarded one of our own assumptions — an A$10–12 token price — when the market disagreed with it.
