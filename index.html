The model

Four revenue lines, one cost stack.

Line 1 · recurring

Sovereign inference

Metered per million tokens. The core line, and the one everything else is sized against.

Line 2 · contracted

Private deployment

Reserved capacity for government, health and enterprise. Priced on access, not tokens.

Line 3 · one-off

Hardware & install

Node sold or placed. Recycles capital and funds fleet growth without dilution.

Line 4 · excluded

Grid & energy

Absorbing midday surplus where it is made. Real, but in none of these numbers.

Gross margin = inference + deployment + hardware − homeowner share − energy − node O&M − platform − company operations

The homeowner takes 20–30% of gross. Energy is near-free at the margin because the node runs on surplus solar the household is otherwise paid 3–6¢ for. Those two facts are what make the rest of this deck work.

Revenue assumption 2 of 2 — throughput

A serving node is not a laptop.

Reviews benchmark these boxes one prompt at a time. A serving node runs hundreds of streams at once and re-uses the same weights read for all of them.

We model 300 tok/s per unit — about a third of benchmarked peak. Real fleets never sit at peak concurrency, and ROCm is less mature than CUDA. Our earlier internal model assumed 95 tok/s, which understated a batched serving node by roughly nine times.

Why they matter

Three of the four need little or no capital and reach households we are already visiting. They carry company overhead while the owned fleet is still being built.

The line we excluded

Grid and VPP services. Real revenue, already licensed by the market — and in none of the numbers here. We will forecast it after a retailer trial, not before.

Why the margin holds

Free electricity isn't the moat.
The free building is.

Energy is roughly a fifth of the cost of inference. If cheap power were the whole story we would have a 20% edge and a hard fight. The advantage is everything a data centre must build that we simply don't.

What a data centre pays for
  • ×Land, and a planning fight to use it
  • ×Building, cooling plant and water
  • ×A grid connection — often a multi-year queue
  • ×Security, redundancy and network
  • ×Electricity at commercial rates, all day
What SunStack pays for
  • ✓A A$2,800 box and a A$400 install
  • ✓A revenue share to the homeowner
  • ✓About A$30 a year of electricity
  • ✓Nothing else — the building, cooling, grid connection and security already exist, and somebody else paid for all four
The line that matters

Over 200× the value
from the same sunshine.

A unit consumes about 394 kWh a year. Exported to the grid at 4¢ those kilowatt-hours earn a household roughly A$16. Turned into tokens, the same energy produces A$4,044 of compute revenue. We are not competing for electrons — we are repricing them.

We need ten thousand.

Our estimate — and how we test it
  • →60% paid utilisation. The biggest lever. Test: a 90-day metered pilot with paying design partners, not letters of intent.
  • →A$1.90/M blended price. Test: signed, invoiced usage showing what the sovereignty premium is actually worth.
  • →300 tok/s on ROCm. Benchmarks are CUDA. Test: reproduce on Ryzen AI Max before buying at volume.
  • →A$1.4M overhead at 1,000 nodes. Test: actual support hours and field-visit costs from the first cohort.

Every number here is a hypothesis with a test attached. We already discarded two of our own assumptions — a 95 tok/s throughput and an A$10–12 token price — when the evidence disagreed.
