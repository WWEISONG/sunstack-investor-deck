The arbitrage

The same kilowatt-hour,
priced three ways.

Australian households are paid 3–6¢ for midday solar they export, and pay 30–40¢ to buy it back after dark. Run through a compute node instead, that same energy produces A$10.27 of inference. We are not competing for electrons — we are repricing them.

Export and retail rates: state regulators, 2026 — many retailers now offer 0¢ at midday. Token value derived from the base case: A$4,044 of revenue from 394 kWh a year.

Why the margin holds

Four of our seven cost lines
are zero.

The building is the moat

A data centre must fund land, a planning fight, the shell, cooling plant, a grid connection often years deep in a queue, and physical security. We fund a box and an install. All four already exist in the home, and somebody else paid for them.

Electricity is the small one

Even the energy edge is modest: a data centre draws 1.52 kWh for every 1 kWh of compute once cooling is counted, at commercial rates. We pay A$30 a year. Real, but not what makes the margin.

Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey, 2026
And we buy cheaper compute

A Ryzen AI Max+ 395 mini PC delivers 91 GB/s of memory bandwidth per A$1,000 against 42 for a DGX Spark — 2.2× more of the thing that actually limits inference, per dollar spent.

Our estimate — and how we test it
  • →60% paid utilisation. The biggest lever. Test: a 90-day metered pilot with paying design partners, not letters of intent.
  • →A$1.90/M blended price. Test: signed, invoiced usage showing what the sovereignty premium is worth.
  • →300 tok/s on ROCm. Benchmarks are CUDA. Test: reproduce on Ryzen AI Max before buying at volume.
  • →The supply funnel filters. Test: conversion rates from the first 500 household approaches.

Every number here is a hypothesis with a test attached. We have already discarded two of our own assumptions — a 95 tok/s throughput and an A$10–12 token price — when the evidence disagreed.
